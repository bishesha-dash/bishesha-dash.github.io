Project 02  /  BD-2026-002

FSAE Suspension Upright

Topology Opt.CNCAl 7075

A topology-optimised front suspension upright machined from Al 7075-T6, cutting unsprung mass 27% while raising stiffness targets across all load cases.

Overview

The problem & the approach

The upright is one of the most load-dense parts on a Formula Student car: it carries braking torque, cornering loads and bump inputs through a palm-sized envelope. This redesign started from tyre-data-derived load cases rather than inherited geometry.

A topology optimisation study in the packaging envelope produced the load paths; the organic result was then re-interpreted as a 3-axis-machinable part to keep manufacturing in-house.

Highlights

  • Load cases derived from tyre data and logged strain telemetry
  • Topology optimisation interpreted for 3-axis CNC manufacturability
  • Validated on-car over a full test season without failure

Analysis & results

Seven load cases were run, with combined braking-plus-cornering proving critical. The optimised design holds a minimum safety factor of 2.1 against yield under the worst case.

Stiffness was treated as a first-class constraint: camber compliance under 1.5 g cornering improved 18% over the previous upright despite the mass reduction.
