Low-Cost Prosthetic Hand

Design for AMMechanismsTPU

A fully 3D-printable, body-powered prosthetic hand built around a whippletree tendon differential, targeting a $40 bill of materials without sacrificing grasp variety.

Overview

The problem & the approach

Commercial body-powered hands cost thousands; this project asks how close a printable design can get on a $40 budget. The core is a whippletree differential that lets four fingers share one tendon while still conforming to objects independently.

Every part prints without supports on a desktop FDM machine, and assembly needs nothing beyond a screwdriver and fishing line — a hard constraint kept from day one for repairability in the field.

Highlights

  • Whippletree differential shares one tendon across four fingers
  • Prints support-free on any desktop FDM printer
  • Complete BOM under $40 including hardware and tendon line

Analysis & results

Cyclic testing of the finger joints ran to 25,000 flex cycles before measurable wear, with living-hinge revisions doubling early prototype life.

Grasp taxonomy coverage was benchmarked against the Cutkosky taxonomy: the hand achieves 11 of 16 grasp types, comparable to devices at ten times the cost.
