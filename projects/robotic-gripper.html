Project 01  /  BD-2026-001

Adaptive Robotic Gripper

SolidWorksFEA3D Printing

An underactuated two-finger gripper designed to grasp irregular objects with a single servo, using compliant linkages tuned through iterative FEA and print-test cycles.

Overview

The problem & the approach

This project set out to answer a simple question: how much grasping versatility can you get from one actuator? The answer turned out to be a four-bar linkage architecture with carefully tuned joint stiffness, letting the fingers passively wrap around whatever they touch.

The design went through four major revisions. Early prototypes gripped well but couldn't release reliably; the final revision adds a torsion-spring return and a redesigned palm geometry that centres objects during approach.

Highlights

  • Single-servo actuation with passive adaptation to object geometry
  • Compliant PLA/TPU hybrid fingers validated against FEA predictions
  • Grasp force tunable from 2 N to 18 N via pre-load adjustment

Analysis & results

Finger linkages were modelled as flexible bodies in ANSYS Static Structural to predict contact force distribution across object diameters from 20 mm to 90 mm.

The FEA drove two geometry changes: thickening the proximal joint web (peak stress dropped 34%) and moving the tendon anchor 6 mm distally for a flatter force curve.
