Project 03  /  BD-2026-003

Compact Heat Exchanger Study

CFDThermalANSYS Fluent

A CFD study of baffle geometry in a compact shell-and-tube heat exchanger, trading baffle cut and spacing to gain 22% heat transfer at constant pumping power.

Overview

The problem & the approach

Shell-side flow in small heat exchangers is notoriously inefficient — large recirculation zones behind each baffle do little work. This study parameterised baffle cut, spacing and tilt to map the trade-off between heat transfer and pressure drop.

Thirty-two Fluent cases were run across the parameter space, with a validated mesh-independence study anchoring the results. The best configuration uses helical-tilted baffles at a 25% cut.

Highlights

  • Parametric study: 32 CFD cases across baffle cut, spacing and tilt
  • Mesh independence verified to under 2% variation on Nusselt number
  • Result validated against Bell-Delaware hand calculations

Analysis & results

The k-omega SST model captured the separated flow behind baffles that a k-epsilon baseline missed entirely — recirculation volume fell 40% in the tilted-baffle design.

At matched pumping power the selected geometry delivers a 22% higher overall heat transfer coefficient, in agreement with Bell-Delaware estimates to within 9%.
