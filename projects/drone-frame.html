Project 06  /  BD-2026-006

Vibration-Tuned Drone Frame

Modal AnalysisCFRPDynamics

A carbon-fibre quadcopter frame designed by modal analysis: arm natural frequencies are placed between motor excitation bands, cutting IMU vibration noise by half.

Overview

The problem & the approach

Frame resonance is the quiet killer of drone flight performance — when an arm's natural frequency sits inside the motor RPM band, the IMU drowns in noise. This frame was designed frequency-first: geometry followed the modal targets.

Arm cross-sections and boom lengths were iterated in a parametric modal study until the first bending mode landed cleanly between hover and full-throttle excitation frequencies, then validated with tap tests on the built frame.

Highlights

  • First arm bending mode placed between motor excitation bands
  • Tap-test validation within 4% of FEA modal predictions
  • IMU vibration noise halved versus the reference frame

Analysis & results

The parametric modal study swept arm width and wall thickness across 24 configurations, tracking the first three modes of each arm assembly with motors mounted.

Flight logs show gyro noise power in the 80-250 Hz band reduced 52% against the commercial reference frame at identical weight, confirming the tap-test results in flight.
